Pigskin Vision: An Adaptive PCA Model for NFL Cornerback Evaluation

Complete Design Process | Seasons 2020 – 2024

Project Overview

This document describes the complete design process for the Pigskin Vision Cornerback Coverage Model—a data-driven system for ranking NFL cornerbacks by season using publicly available statistics. The model takes a season year as input and returns a ranked list of qualified cornerbacks based on their coverage performance, with feature weightages that adapt automatically to the statistical trends of each individual season.

The final model covers seasons 2020 through 2024, requires no manual labels or human rankings, and uses Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to derive adaptive weightages from the data itself. It filters to CBs who played at least 13 games, 600 defensive snaps, and received at least 40 targets in a given season.

Phase 1: Problem Definition

Why Cornerbacks Are Hard to Rank

The starting point of this project was recognizing that cornerback evaluation is uniquely difficult in the NFL. Unlike quarterbacks or running backs, great cornerbacks are often defined by what does not happen—passes that are never thrown, catches that are never made. The traditional box score offers almost nothing useful: interceptions are rare and often luck-dependent, tackles mean the receiver already caught the ball, and pass breakups are inconsistently tracked.

The core question driving the project was: how often are CBs thrown at, and what happens when the ball comes their way? This framing suggested a per-target rate approach—normalizing all outcomes by the number of times a CB was actually targeted, rather than by snaps or games played.

Phase 2: Data Source Selection

The nflfastR Flaw

The first data source considered was nflfastR play-by-play data. While this dataset tags defending players, a diagnostic check revealed a critical flaw: only 10.2% of regular-season pass plays had a defender tagged. The pass_defense columns are only populated when an official NFL statistic is credited to a defender—primarily pass breakups and interceptions—not on every coverage snap. This meant we were measuring impactful plays rather than true targets.

PFR Advanced Defense Stats (Final Source)

The final model utilizes Pro Football Reference (PFR) Advanced Defense Statistics. PFR uses a manual charting methodology that tracks every targeted pass by the intended coverage defender—not just plays with official NFL statistics. This is the gold standard for CB target data.

Phase 3: Feature Engineering

The model evaluates cornerbacks based on five core features, all computed as per-target rates to normalize for playing time and opportunity. All features are oriented so that higher values indicate better coverage quality:

  • Incompletion Rate: (targets - completions - INTs) / targets. Captures coverage success on challenge plays.
  • Interception Rate: interceptions / targets. Captures ball-hawking ability.
  • Passer Rating Allowed (Inv): 158.3 - avg_passer_rating_allowed. The most comprehensive efficiency metric.
  • Target Rate (Inv): 1 - (targets / season_snaps). The "Reputation" metric—elite CBs are actively avoided by QBs.
  • Yards Per Target (Inv/Winsorized): 1 / (winsorized_yds_per_tgt + 1). Measures yardage efficiency, adjusted to prevent extreme distortion.

Phase 4: Qualification Filters & Outlier Management

To ensure the rankings were not skewed by small sample sizes, three strict filters were applied: 40+ Targets, 600+ Defensive Snaps, and 13+ Games Played. The games filter directly addresses injury-shortened outliers. For instance, Bryce Callahan (2020) was correctly excluded by this filter after posting elite stats in only 10 games.

Even with filters, extreme outliers exist. To solve this, continuous variables like "Yards Per Target" are winsorized at the 5th and 95th percentiles, capping statistical anomalies without removing the player entirely.

Phase 5: Model Architecture: The PCA Engine

The project utilizes Principal Component Analysis (PCA)—an unsupervised machine learning algorithm. PCA finds the linear combination of features that explains the most variance in each individual season's data.

Features are standardized to zero mean and unit variance using the current season's data. This forces the PCA weightages to adapt to the statistical landscape of the league. In interception-heavy years, INT rate holds more weight; in low-turnover years, the weight drops automatically.

Phase 6: Validation & Key Results (2019-2025)

The model’s output successfully tracks with elite historical performances and modern consensus without requiring manual human adjustment:

  • 2019: Stephon Gilmore (#2) won DPOY; Tre'Davious White (#3) was statistically elite.
  • 2020: Xavien Howard (#1) led the NFL with 10 interceptions.
  • 2021: Jamel Dean (#1) posted an elite 4.8 yards per target. Trevon Diggs (#10) reflected high INTs offset by high yardage allowed.
  • 2024: The model is exceptionally strong here, with Derek Stingley Jr. (#1) and Patrick Surtain (#2) matching expert consensus perfectly.
  • 2025: Joey Porter Jr. (#1) and Quinyon Mitchell (#2) identified as early breakout stars under relaxed filters.

Conclusion & Known Limitations

By utilizing per-target rates and era-adaptive PCA, the Pigskin Vision model provides a transparent, data-driven ranking system that bypasses the limitations of the traditional box score. The primary limitation remains the inability to measure separation prevention (preventing separation before the ball is thrown) or assignment difficulty (shadowing WR1s vs. covering a slot receiver). Addressing these would require proprietary tracking data like NFL Next Gen Stats.
