Pigskin Vision: An Adaptive PCA Model for NFL Cornerback Evaluation

Complete Design Process | Seasons 2019 – 2025

Project Overview

This document describes the complete design process for the Pigskin Vision Cornerback Coverage Model — a data-driven system for ranking NFL cornerbacks by season using publicly available statistics. The model takes a season year as input and returns a ranked list of qualified cornerbacks based on their coverage performance, with feature weightages that adapt automatically to the statistical trends of each individual season.

The final model covers seasons 2019 through 2025, requires no manual labels or human rankings, and uses Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to derive adaptive weightages from the data itself. It filters to CBs who played at least 13 games, 600 defensive snaps, and received at least 40 targets in a given season.

Phase 1: Problem Definition

The starting point of this project was recognizing that cornerback evaluation is uniquely difficult in the NFL. Unlike quarterbacks or running backs, great cornerbacks are often defined by what does not happen — passes that are never thrown and catches that are never made. The traditional box score offers almost nothing useful: interceptions are rare and often luck-dependent, while tackles usually mean the receiver has already caught the ball.

The core question driving the project was: how often are CBs thrown at, and what happens when the ball comes their way? This framing immediately suggested a per-target rate approach — normalizing all outcomes by the number of times a CB was actually targeted, rather than by snaps or games played. This allows us to see who is truly efficient in coverage regardless of their team's defensive scheme.

Phase 2: Data Source Selection

The first data source considered was nflfastR play-by-play data. However, a diagnostic check revealed a critical flaw: only 10.2% of regular season pass plays had a defender tagged. The columns are only populated when an official statistic is credited, meaning we were measuring impactful plays rather than true coverage consistency.

The solution was Pro Football Reference (PFR) advanced defense statistics. PFR uses manual charting methodology that tracks every targeted pass by the intended coverage defender — not just plays with official NFL statistics. This provided the necessary granularity to see which defenders were being targeted and how they responded on every single play.

Phase 3: Feature Engineering

All features in this model are computed as per-target rates rather than raw counts. This normalizes for playing time and opportunity. A CB who plays 800 snaps and allows 40 completions might look worse than one who plays 400 snaps and allows 30, but the per-target completion rate reveals who is actually the more effective lockdown defender.

The model utilizes five key features: Incompletion Rate, Interception Rate, Passer Rating Allowed (Inverted), Target Rate (Inverted), and Yards Per Target (Inverted). All features are oriented so that higher values represent better coverage performance, allowing the PCA to aggregate them into a single coherent score.

Phase 4: Qualification Filters

Three filters are applied to ensure rankings are based on meaningful samples: 40+ Targets, 600+ Defensive Snaps, and 13+ Games Played. Without these filters, a CB with only 5 targets could show a perfect 0.0 passer rating based purely on luck, which would unfairly place them at the top of the rankings over true starters.

The 13-game filter was added specifically to address injury-shortened seasons like Bryce Callahan's 2020 campaign. He played 10 games and posted the lowest yards per target in the dataset, which ranked him #1 before the filter was applied. By requiring 13 games, the model ensures it is ranking "full-season" performances that withstood the grind of an NFL schedule.

Phase 5: Model Architecture

The project initially explored supervised machine learning, but this was abandoned because it inherited human bias from the "expert" labels used for training. Instead, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was chosen. As an unsupervised algorithm, it finds the linear combination of features that explains the most variance in each season independently.

Because the model normalizes features within each season, the weightages adapt automatically. In high-turnover years, interceptions carry more weight; in low-turnover years, the model naturally shifts focus toward incompletion rates and yardage prevention. This makes the model era-adaptive and robust against changing NFL passing trends.

Phase 6: Validation and Results

The model's results align closely with expert consensus and historical context. In 2019, Stephon Gilmore ranked #2 during his Defensive Player of the Year campaign. In 2024, the model successfully identified Derek Stingley Jr. and Patrick Surtain as the top two cornerbacks in the league, reflecting their elite statistical dominance.

Crucially, the model also correctly identifies "boom-or-bust" styles. For example, Trevon Diggs in 2021 ranked #10—a high mark, but one that accounts for the high yardage he allowed despite his league-leading 11 interceptions. This balance between big plays and consistent coverage is the hallmark of the PCA approach.

Conclusion

The Pigskin Vision Cornerback Coverage Model successfully addresses the core challenge of CB evaluation: deriving meaningful performance rankings from statistics that only partially capture true coverage quality.

By combining per-target rate features with season-level normalization and unsupervised PCA, the model produces rankings that adapt to each season's statistical environment without requiring any manual configuration.
